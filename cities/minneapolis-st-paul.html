Minneapolis–St. Paul notary after 5 and on weekends

The search everyone runs on Sunday night. Three real paths: video notary from the couch, a cheap walk-in if you can get there while a human with a stamp is actually on shift, or a mobile notary who drives to you.

Tonight, from the couch

Remote online notarization (RON) is legal in Minnesota for many document types. Proof/Notarize and OneNotary run 24/7. Typical consumer price about $25 a session. Confirm the receiving party accepts RON before you pay.

Cheap walk-in

Your own bank is often free. UPS Store is the usual paid walk-in. Notary hours are shorter than store hours, and staff will turn you away in the last hour or if they are alone. Call the specific store.

Someone comes to you

Twin Cities mobile notaries advertise evenings, hospitals, and same-day. Expect a travel fee on top of the state notarial fee. Good when you cannot leave, bad when you only need one cheap stamp.

Hours on store websites are often wrong for the notary, not the store. A notary can be out, on lunch, or refuse work in the last hour. Remote online notarization is legal in Minnesota for many document types — not all. If a bank, title company, or court demands wet ink, stay in person.

Minnesota caps what a notary may charge per notarial act. Travel and “convenience” fees sit outside that cap. Ask for the split before anyone drives.
